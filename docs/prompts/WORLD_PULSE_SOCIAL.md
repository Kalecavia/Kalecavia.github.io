## Expanded prompt

Create a premium Open Graph social-preview image for World Pulse, designed as
a cinematic editorial visualization of the planet’s realtime pulse as one
connected world machine.

Master canvas: 2400 × 1260 px, landscape, 1.91:1 aspect ratio. Compose for
reliable Open Graph cropping across social platforms. No text or branding
inside the image.

Place a large abstract three-dimensional Earth slightly right of center,
occupying roughly 52–58% of the canvas height. Keep the entire left third
predominantly dark and visually quiet, with generous negative space suitable
for an optional title overlay. The planet should feel suspended in a vast,
deep spatial environment, with a clear foreground, midground and background.
Avoid symmetrical centering and avoid filling the frame with interface
elements.

Construct the Earth as an elegant, semi-transparent global system rather than
a conventional globe. Use refined geodetic linework, faint latitude-like
structural arcs without literal borders, pulsing data nodes, thin long-distance
network curves, subtle reservoirs, circulation chambers and flowing energy
channels embedded within and around the sphere. Suggest ecological, social,
infrastructural and planetary systems functioning as one interconnected
organism. The forms should feel engineered, scientifically plausible and
editorially meaningful, not decorative or game-like.

Do not show national borders, country outlines, labels, icons, maps, dashboards
or readable interface graphics. Continental presence may be suggested only
through density, terrain-like flow patterns and system topology, never through
political boundaries.

Create strong dimensional separation through layered line density, volumetric
haze, restrained atmospheric scattering and soft occlusion. Some network arcs
may extend toward the viewer while others disappear behind the globe. Add a
tactile atmospheric shell around the planet with subtle particulate matter,
fine vapor, suspended dust and faint energetic currents. The result should feel
touchable, spatial and physically present rather than flat or holographic.

Use a cinematic editorial camera positioned slightly above the planet’s
equator, viewing the globe at a subtle three-quarter angle. Simulate the visual

elegant perspective, controlled depth, no wide-angle distortion. Keep the
planet sharply resolved with a gentle falloff into the surrounding atmosphere.
Preserve clean silhouettes and ample breathing room around the sphere.

Use high-end, directional, sculptural lighting. A cool cyan key light should

heavy backlight, harsh lens flares or nightclub neon.

Render the globe using a sophisticated combination of dark translucent mineral
glass, fine metallic filaments, matte carbon-like structural surfaces,
suspended luminous particles and subtle fluid-energy volumes. Materials should
show believable reflection, refraction, micro-texture, edge wear and imperfect
density variation. Keep emissive elements thin, precise and embedded in the
structure rather than floating as generic glowing effects.

Color palette: deep ink and near-black (#05070A to #0B1016), electric cyan
(#20D8F2), controlled ecosystem green (#45B878), a sparingly used coral-red
urgency accent (#E65F52), and warm ivory highlights (#F3EBDD). Maintain
disciplined color separation, rich blacks and premium contrast. Do not
introduce magenta, purple-heavy cyberpunk tones or rainbow gradients.

Top-tier interactive documentary art direction with the authority of a global
science, climate or systems publication. Cinematic, intelligent, premium,
spatial and editorially credible. Prioritize composition, hierarchy, depth and
material realism over visual density.

Negative prompt: no text, letters, numbers, captions, logos, symbols,
watermarks, UI labels, national borders, flags, map graphics, dashboard, HUD,

negative space on the left.

Final requirement: a sophisticated, text-free, cinematic 3D world-system
visualization in 2400 × 1260 px, 1.91:1 landscape, with the planet slightly
right of center, premium editorial realism, disciplined color, strong depth and
a calm, dark title-safe area on the left.
